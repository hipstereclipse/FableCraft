# FableCraft World Reference: Locations of Fable: The Lost Chapters (2005)

A buildable architectural, spatial, and atmospheric reference for recreating every region of Albion in the FableCraft Minecraft mod.

## TL;DR
- *Fable: The Lost Chapters* contains roughly 30+ discrete loading-zone "regions," organized into clusters (Heroes' Guild, Greatwood, Darkwood, Bowerstone, Witchwood island, Lychfield/Bargate, Oakvale, Hook Coast, and the TLC-exclusive Northern Wastes), connected by paths, gates, bridges, and Cullis Gate teleporters rather than one seamless overworld.
- The **Heroes' Guild** is the signature hub: a grey-stone fortress with a central chalk-stream courtyard, two bridges to a Will-training island, melee ring and archery range, a fortress hall (Map Room, library, dining pub, dormitories, Maze's Tower), a Demon Door, and an underground cave bridge leading to the domed, fresco-walled Chamber of Fate.
- TLC adds **nine new areas and sixteen additional quests** over the original Fable - most importantly the frozen **Northern Wastes** (Lost Bay, foothills, Snowspire Village with its Oracle, the haunted Necropolis, Archon's Shrine, and the volcanic Archon's Folly behind the Bronze Gate) plus the Darkwood Bordello - all reached via the Hook Coast lighthouse and the Ship of the Drowned.

---

## PART 1 - WORLD OVERVIEW & TOPOLOGY

Albion in *Fable: TLC* is NOT a seamless open world. It is a network of self-contained "regions," each its own loading zone, joined at fixed transition gates/paths. Travel between distant clusters is by **Cullis Gate** teleporters (round magical stone platforms that emit a blue glow) once activated, accessed via the Guild Seal map. According to The Fable Wiki, much of the TLC content "was meant to be in the original Fable...but due to time constraints, the developers ended up merging some of those ideas together" - and specifically, "Snowspire and the Necropolis were meant to be their own island and not connected to each other."

The overall geography: Albion is a green, sea-bounded land. The southern coast holds **Oakvale**; the interior is dominated by three large forests running roughly south-to-north - **Darkwood** (dark, twisted, marshy; southernmost interior), **Greatwood** (bright, sprawling forest; central), with **Lookout Point** as the northern hub. **Bowerstone**, the largest town, sits just north of the Guild. **Witchwood** is a misty evergreen island off the coast (home of Knothole Glade and the Arena). To the north lie **Lychfield** (graveyards) and **Bargate** (the prison island). **Hook Coast** is a snowy island in the far north, and the TLC-exclusive **Northern Wastes** lie across icy seas beyond it.

### Region cluster breakdown and connections:

**Heroes' Guild cluster:** Guild of Heroes (main hall + grounds) -> Guild Woods (east) and Guild Caves -> Chamber of Fate (underground). The Guild's main exit leads to **Lookout Point**.

**Lookout Point** is the central mainland hub - "the first region you come across after leaving the Heroes' Guild." It connects to: the Heroes' Guild, Bowerstone South (north, over a bridge), the Picnic Area, and Greatwood Entrance (south). It is the safest area in the game (no hostile spawns), with a rotating pointing statue, a Boasting Platform, and a title vendor.

**Bowerstone cluster:** Bowerstone South (slums/market, reached from Lookout Point) -> Bowerstone North (wealthy district, gated, accessible only after the Arena) and Bowerstone Quay (harbor). Sub-areas: Bowerstone Jail, Tavern Cellar, Bowerstone Manor (Lady Grey's), Lady Grey's Bedroom.

**Greatwood cluster:** Greatwood Entrance (south of Lookout Point) branches to Fisher Creek, Orchard Farm, Greatwood Lake -> Greatwood Gorge (bandit toll bridge) -> Greatwood Cullis Gate -> Greatwood Caves (which houses the Hobbe Caves) -> leads south to Darkwood. Also Rose Cottage (Demon Door garden).

**Darkwood cluster:** Darkwood Entrance (from Greatwood Caves) -> Darkwood Marshes, Darkwood Lake, Darkwood Camp (merchant sanctuary), Darkwood Bordello (TLC only), Darkwood Weir, Ancient Cullis Gate, and the Chapel of Skorm. Darkwood connects south toward Barrow Fields and Oakvale.

**Oakvale cluster:** Oakvale (coastal village) -> Barrow Fields, Memorial Garden, Clifftop Path (leads to the Bandit Camp), Grey House and Grey House Cellar.

**Bandit Camp cluster:** Abandoned Road -> Twinblade's Camp -> Twinblade's Elite Camp -> Twinblade's Tent (reached from Oakvale's Clifftop Path).

**Witchwood island cluster:** reached via Witchwood Cullis Gate -> Witchwood Stones (and Witchwood Cavern behind the Demon Door) -> Temple of Avo -> Witchwood Lake -> Knothole Glade (village) and the Arena Entrance -> Arena -> Hall of Heroes / Arena Waiting Area.

**Lychfield & Bargate cluster:** Lychfield Graveyard (north of Bowerstone) with its gated Demon Door -> Old Graveyard Path -> Circle of the Dead, Underground Chamber/Tunnel/Passage -> Bargate Prison (on its isolated northern island). Also Cliffside Path, Windmill Hill, Headsman's Hill, Headsman's Cave, Gibbet Woods, Prison Path.

**Hook Coast:** snowy northern island, reached by Cullis Gate; site of the lighthouse. After the Fire Heart is placed, the Ship of the Drowned departs from here to the Northern Wastes.

**Northern Wastes (TLC only):** Lost Bay (arrival port) -> Northern Wastes Foothills -> branches to the Necropolis (northernmost gate) and Archon's Shrine -> Snowspire Village (with the Oracle) -> Bronze Gate -> Archon's Folly (volcanic final-boss arena).

## PART 2 - DETAILED HUB DESCRIPTIONS

### THE HEROES' GUILD (most detailed treatment)

**Lore flavor:** Founded by the bandit-turned-leader Nostro under the tutelage of the immortal Scythe, the Guild trains apprentices into Heroes. It is the player's home base and tutorial area, and the place quests are dispensed. Theresa describes it as an academy that "for centuries trained the most supremely gifted sons and daughters of Albion."

**Overall campus & architectural style:** The Guild "was built as a fortress and sanctuary." Its defining material is **heavy, grey stone-masonry walls** - thick, defensible curtain walls that have withstood bandit attacks. The style is medieval grey-stone fortress. The campus is extensive ("the scope of the Guild acreage quite extensive"), arranged around a central grounds/courtyard with a chalk stream running through it, an orchard, and its own woods. The grounds have an idyllic, pastoral daytime feel - green lawns, water, apple trees, fluttering butterflies - and a canonical dawn/golden-hour lighting state (the "Sunrise in the Heroes' Guild" screenshot). Lookout Point and the Guild gardens are the only TLC areas that play the track **"Summer Fields" by composer Russell Shaw**, which he wrote for "a kind of hub that connects three or four of the main regions," to the keywords "Safe, Summery, Idyllic, relaxed," performed free-form (without a click track) by the Philharmonia Orchestra - use this as the tonal target for the grounds.

**The main Guild Hall (interior):** A single large stone interior subdivided by function:
- **Western side:** the Guildmaster's usual spot, the **quest map**, the **experience shrine** (spending platform), a **Cullis Gate** platform, the **staircase to the upper floor**, and the **exit to Lookout Point**.
- **Eastern side:** the **dining hall / pub**, run by Alfie the innkeeper. A **statue resembling Theresa** stands at the entrance to the Guild Tavern. The Guild is said to be inspired by an English pub called "The Guild," explaining the in-hall pub.
- **Southern side:** the **Guild Shop / General Store** (Dylan the shopkeeper), selling melee and ranged weapons plus some clothing and produce.
- **Northern side:** the **Library** (tall bookshelves lining the walls, holding lore books) -> which contains the bookcase-hidden door leading to the **Guild Caves and Chamber of Fate**.

**The Map Room:** Located "just beyond the front vestibule and at the heart of the guild's main floor," it contains a **large three-dimensional relief map of Albion** (the quest map table). Quest Cards are accepted here, and the Guildmaster is usually found here. Stairs lead from the Map Room up to the Guild Shop level.

**Upstairs / dormitories:** The staircase on the western side leads to the upper floor, which holds **three dormitory rooms** with apprentice beds and bookshelves - the sleeping quarters.

**Maze's Tower:** A distinct **separate tower** structure on the campus, with an upper room serving as Maze's quarters (bookshelves with lore books). Build as a vertical stone tower attached to the complex.

**Servants' quarters:** Servant lodging is built into a **hillside** behind the main structures (three beds inside the hill, normally inaccessible).

**The grounds / courtyard:** The heart of the outdoor campus is a **chalk stream flowing through the midst of the property**. A **small island sits in the middle of the stream, reached by two bridges** - this island holds the **Will (magic) training area** with "state-of-the-art magic-response dummies" for practicing lightning. Flanking the courtyard:
- **Melee training ring** - with a straw dummy for hand-to-hand/weapon practice.
- **Archery range** - with both moving and stationary targets.
- **Apple orchard** - apples lie scattered on the grounds to be picked up.
- **Demon Door "The Library Arcanum"** near the Will area: in Fable/TLC, Demon Doors appear as a **bald, strong-looking bearded man's face carved into stone**. This one speaks the full canonical riddle: *"Your path is dark. Only a light will reveal it. And you are not bright enough."* (Solution: turn on the lamp.) Inside is a **mystic library set in a grove, with bookshelves, chairs, and a small pond with butterflies** (rewards: the books *Making Friends* and *Book of Spells*, the Howl Tattoo, and an Elixir of Life).
- **Silver Key Chest requiring 20 silver keys** - the hardest chest to open in the original Fable; it contains the legendary Murren Greathammer (note: in the original Fable this weapon becomes permanently unavailable once the "Try to Stop Jack of Blades" quest card is accepted, so it should be opened beforehand).
- **Graves of legendary heroes** - upright standing-stone grave markers (notably tall/monolithic in shape).
- Fishing ripples in the stream.

**The Guild Woods (east of the Guild):** "A small forested area just east of the Heroes' Guild," serene and contrasting the training grounds. Features **several pools of water**, **scattered Old Kingdom stone ruins** dotting the area, deciduous trees, and **butterflies**. A beetle nest sits on the east side. It has no background music (pure ambient woodland). A path/bridge connects it back to the Guild grounds. A stream with fishing ripples runs through it.

**The Guild Caves -> Chamber of Fate (underground):** From the Library's bookcase door, a walkway leads into the **Guild Caves** - "a bridge inside a large cave," a long stone span through a barren cavern. This leads to the **Chamber of Fate**, the oldest part of the Guild: a **massive circular, domed stone chamber** (its minimap file is literally named "FRESCODOME"). The **center of the room is slightly elevated, with steps leading up to a raised central platform**; the highest point is where a swirling vortex appears after Jack is defeated (and where the Sword of Aeons sits). The surrounding walls bear **fresco mural panels** depicting the Hero's accomplishments (the Oakvale raid, the Arena, Bargate Prison, the Kraken, the dragon, etc.). A **Cullis Gate** (round raised stone disc with blue magical glow) serves the chamber. This is the only part of the Guild that survives into Fable II.

**Signature recognizable elements for builders:** the grey-stone fortress hall; the 3D Albion map table; the chalk stream with two bridges to the Will island; the bearded stone Demon Door; the domed fresco Chamber of Fate; Maze's tower.

### OAKVALE (coastal starting village)

**Lore flavor:** The Hero's home town, a secluded southern seaside farming-and-fishing community. Raided and burned by bandits early in the story, later rebuilt.

**Layout & architecture:** A seaside village set "amongst the cliffs by the sea," built on a rise above the beach. Coastal-village architecture: modest cottages with thatched/tiled roofs, a guarded **gate**, and a **central area arranged around a large tree** with the shops surrounding it. In its early (childhood) period the village had only four houses, a general shop, a weapons store, and a tavern. There is a **well** with a ring of houses around it, and the rebuilt town adds more houses (seven-plus). A **small tilled field with a scarecrow** lies west of the gates; **three barns** for food storage sit to the southeast.

**Coast & docks:** The village steps down to a **beach** with a **pier/dock (quay)** that is guarded (weapons not allowed in town). Ruined boats and a jetty sit on the western beach. The shore wraps around with paths.

**Key buildings:** the **tavern**, **general store**, **weapons shop**, a **tattoo shop**. The **Memorial Garden** (graveyard) sits up a path on the eastern side, containing a statue of a hero swinging a giant axe and many graves. Oakvale's **Demon Door** (Serenity Farm interior - wants an act of pure love) is on the grounds. The town is surrounded by fences/low walls and has a guarded gate. TLC adds the Chicken Kicking Competition on the beach.

**Atmosphere:** Warm, idyllic coastal palette - sea blues, sandy beach, green fields, wooden and stone cottages; bright daytime. Connection points: paths to Barrow Fields, Memorial Garden, and the Clifftop Path (to the Bandit Camp).

### BOWERSTONE (largest town)

**Lore flavor:** Albion's economic center and largest town, just north of the Guild, ruled by Mayor Lady Elvira Grey. Surrounded by a large defensive wall and heavily guarded; weapons are confiscated at entry. Sharply class-divided.

**Bowerstone South (the slums/market):** Reached from Lookout Point over a bridge. The main market district - a **tavern, general store, clothing & armour shop, weapons smith, a barber, several houses, and the only school in Albion**. Many residents are poor, living in **shed-like houses**. Chickens roam. Architecture is denser, more urban than the villages, with multi-story timber-and-stone buildings, cobbled streets, and a town-square feel. The Tavern Cellar is behind/beneath the tavern. Connects to Bowerstone North and Bowerstone Quay.

**Bowerstone North (wealthy district):** Gated off - the player cannot enter until completing the Arena (Lady Grey's invitation). The prosperous side: finer, larger houses, a pub, one shop (sells the Solus Greatsword), and **Bowerstone Manor** (Lady Grey's residence) - the grandest building, a proper manor house. A class barrier is enforced: South residents can't enter North without invitation. The town's temporary prison cell (Bowerstone Jail) sits outside the northern walls.

**Bowerstone Quay (harbor):** Bowerstone's only harbor, on the river; primarily used for commerce with Oakvale (goods sail down the river and out the delta). It has barns, a hut, and (in TLC) a witch's potion hut. The local Fist Fighters gang meets here.

**Walls, gates, bridge, river:** The whole town is ringed by a large wall with guarded gates. The **River Bower** runs through, crossed by a bridge; the quay sits on the water. Guards (led by a Sheriff) patrol throughout.

**Atmosphere:** Grey-brown urban palette, stone walls, timber-framed buildings; busier and more crowded than rural areas. The class divide is visible architecturally - ramshackle sheds in the South vs. clean manor houses in the North.

### KNOTHOLE GLADE (hidden forest village)

**Lore flavor:** A village founded by woodcutters of old, on the misty Witchwood island, near the Arena and Temple of Avo. Famed for balverine hunters (birthplace of Scarlet Robe). Led by a Chieftain. Constantly threatened by balverines and bandits - the only town where weapons may be openly carried.

**Layout & architecture:** A village tucked among cliffs, protected by a **large wooden gate that is constantly guarded** - the secret/secure entrance. The architecture is rustic **wood-based, Norse-and-Celtic** in style: per The Fable Wiki, "Witchwood appears to be based on Ireland during the Celtic period due to the architecture of its main town, Knothole Glade," and concept artist Pete Amachree confirms of the villagers, "Norse and Celtic was what the art director wanted." Expect wooden huts, totem-like carvings, and a tribal feel. In the **middle of the town stands a statue of Scarlet Robe** (the heroine).

**Buildings:** a tavern, a weapons shop, a tattoo shop, a general store, the Chief's hut, a bard, several buyable houses (including marital homes at the back of town), and a **shooting range** (a unique feature). A **Demon Door** here opens only when shot with a strong bow, leading to the Hidden Copse. The village backs onto cliffs with a hill at one end.

**Atmosphere:** Misty, evergreen, damp forest setting; greens and browns, wood smoke; enclosed and sheltered by surrounding cliffs and forest. Connects via Witchwood Lake to the Arena Entrance and Temple of Avo.

---

### HOOK COAST (northern port town)

**Lore flavor:** A snowy fishing town on an isolated northern island - the only intact remnant of the Old Kingdom, protected long ago by a barrier cast by its monks (who died creating it, leaving imprints where they stood). Economy based on fishing. Reached only by Cullis Gate.

**Layout & architecture:** The only snow-covered town in the base game. A coastal fishing settlement with **rows of houses, a tavern, a weapon shop**, stairs connecting tiers of the town, and a graveyard (where lighthouse keepers and monks are buried, with a notable bell at the end of the northern row of houses). The dominant landmark is the **lighthouse**, located in the bottom-right of the map by the harbor. The town also contains the **ruined Abbey** with a magical barrier at its entrance (the monastery the monks built). A Guild presence/outpost connects here via the story.

**The lighthouse:** A tall stone tower with an interior stair to the top; the Fire Heart is placed at its summit to summon the Ship of the Drowned. The climactic Maze fight ranges across the cemetery, the shops, and up the lighthouse.

**Destroyed/later state:** During "Return to Hook Coast," Screamers drive out the villagers (the town is evacuated). The town is shown abandoned, with the magical barrier sealing the Abbey until the Guildmaster dispels it.

**Atmosphere:** Cold, snowy, grey-white and slate-blue palette; sea spray, snow-laden roofs, the rotating lighthouse beam. Quiet and isolated. The architecture echoes the Old Kingdom (older stonework than mainland towns).

## PART 3 - REMAINING REGIONS (concise build notes)

**Lookout Point:** Open grassy hilltop hub with a pointing statue (rotates like a clock), a Boasting Platform, title vendor, two guards at the Bowerstone gate. Safest area; peaceful palette. Hub connecting Guild, Bowerstone South, Picnic Area, Greatwood.

**Greatwood region:** Bright, sprawling green forest. *Greatwood Entrance* (open area with fences, paths branching to Fisher Creek/Orchard Farm/Greatwood Lake). *Greatwood Lake* (large lake, bandits and trolls). *Greatwood Gorge* (a gorge with a bandit toll bridge and stairs; Demon Door "Arboretum"). *Greatwood Cullis Gate* (teleporter clearing). *Greatwood Caves* (rocky tunnels separating Greatwood from Darkwood; houses the Hobbe Caves entrance and the "Butterfly House" Demon Door; sign reads "Abandon hope all ye who enter here"). *Orchard Farm* (apple/cider farm with barns). *Fisher Creek* (a fisherman's hut where the creek flows to the sea). *Rose Cottage* (small house with a Demon Door in its garden).

**Hobbe Cave:** A dungeon off Greatwood Caves - dark stone tunnels, the archetypal Fable dungeon, infested with Hobbes.

**Darkwood region:** Dark, twisted, mist- and marsh-filled forest. *Darkwood Entrance* (first area from Greatwood Caves). *Darkwood Marshes* (boggy, foggy wetland). *Darkwood Lake* (dark water). *Darkwood Camp* (a small fenced merchant sanctuary - one of only two safe spots). *Darkwood Bordello* (TLC only - a safe-haven building with a Demon Door; can be run for profit or converted to a women's refuge). *Darkwood Weir*. *Ancient Cullis Gate* (teleporter). *Chapel of Skorm* (evil sacrificial altar; one of three regions switching between two songs). Palette: murky greys, browns, dead trees, fog.

**Twinblade's Camp / Bandit Camp:** Reached via Oakvale's Clifftop Path and the Abandoned Road. A sprawl of **bandit tents and palisades** by a lake, with three stalls and a small makeshift tavern. Progresses inward through gated checkpoints: Twinblade's Camp -> Twinblade's Elite Camp -> **Twinblade's Tent** (the Bandit King's large tent, with a fighting circle surrounded by bandits, and chests inside). Rough wooden fences, campfires, hide tents.

**Witchwood region:** Misty evergreen island. *Witchwood Cullis Gate* (arrival teleporter). *Witchwood Stones* (Old Kingdom standing stones / singing stones that spell out a Demon Door's name "HITS"; the Witchwood Cavern lies behind the Demon Door). *Temple of Avo* (a small good-aligned temple with a circular donation fountain and two black-robed acolytes). *Witchwood Lake* (small shallow lake between Knothole Glade, Arena Entrance, and Temple of Avo).

**The Arena (Witchwood Arena):** A giant Roman-style **Colosseum** commissioned by Nostro - a large circular amphitheater with tiered spectator seating and statues looking down into the arena floor. *Arena Entrance* is a path where crowds gather, with a title vendor. The *Arena Waiting Area* / preparation room has practice dummies and a supply shop. The *Hall of Heroes* adjoins it. Eight rounds of combat take place in the central ring.

**Lychfield region:** A dilapidated, dead land north of Bowerstone. *Lychfield Graveyard* - crypts, sarcophagi, tombstones, pools of water, a Gravekeeper's hut, and Nostro's crypt. A **Demon Door at the gate stairs** guards the only access to the Old Graveyard Path and opens only at Nostro's command. *Old Graveyard Path* (dead vegetation, rotten air). *Circle of the Dead* (a ritual circle where undead spawn). *Underground Chamber/Tunnel/Passage* lead toward Bargate. Endless undead spawn here. Palette: grey, decayed, foggy, green-tinged.

**Bargate region & Prison:** *Cliffside Path*, *Windmill Hill* (with a windmill), *Headsman's Hill*, *Headsman's Cave*, *Gibbet Woods*, *Prison Path*. **Bargate Prison** sits on an **isolated northern island** - a stone fortress-prison composed of a courtyard, ramparts, three cell blocks (two explorable), a barracks, a torture chamber, and the warden's office, all bearing Bowerstone crests. An Underground Chamber beneath once held a Kraken.

**Grey House:** A house (with cellar) in the Oakvale area associated with Lady Grey's family backstory; scene of undead in the Murder with a Twist / Investigating the Mayor quest line.

### TLC-EXCLUSIVE NORTHERN WASTES

**Lost Bay:** The arrival port from the Ship of the Drowned - a long-disused, snow-covered harbor with an abandoned building, ruined docks, a 30-key Silver Chest, and a statue commemorating Archon's ancient soldiers. Snowy, desolate, icy seas. The "northernmost port of the Old Kingdom."

**Northern Wastes Foothills:** Hills under constant blizzard leading up from the Lost Bay, splitting toward the Necropolis and Snowspire. Perilous, balverine-infested, snow-blasted. The northernmost gate leads to the Necropolis.

**Necropolis:** A haunted, ruined ghost-city - once a thriving Old Kingdom town, now rubble. Crumbling buildings, graves, a broken bridge over a river, roaming ghosts (who murmur, unaware they're dead) and zombies, plus Frost Balverines, Wraiths, and an ice troll. The four Glyphs of Inquiry are buried/dug up here. Desolate, frozen, gothic-ruin palette.

**Archon's Shrine:** A region between the Foothills and Snowspire dominated by a **large domed shrine**. In its center, the Hero places three Hero souls into stones to open the adjacent **Bronze Gate**. Contains a Cullis Gate and chests. A plaque notes it dates to the Kingdom of Archon and was used to mystically lock the Bronze Gate.

**Bronze Gate:** A huge ancient door marking the northernmost barrier of the inhabited Wastes; legend says opening it brings the end of the world. Requires three Hero souls. It is the entrance to the final boss area.

**Snowspire Village:** The only remaining settlement in the Northern Wastes - a snowy village similar in feel to Hook Coast. Founded in the Old Kingdom, reduced over centuries from a great city to a village by invading hordes. Weapons illegal here (a guarded town). Contains a Charity Shop, a buyable house, villagers, and at the far side the **Oracle** - an enormous ancient stone monument/face carved from a giant slab of rock, which answers the Hero's questions. The Necropolis can be seen on the southern horizon (a 2D backdrop in TLC). Snow-laden, blue-white, isolated.

**Archon's Folly (Dragon Cliff):** The northernmost point of Albion, behind the Bronze Gate - the final-boss arena. A **medium-sized rock platform surrounded by lava** in an infernal, volcanic setting. Here the Hero fights Jack of Blades' dragon form and chooses whether to cast Jack's mask into the lava. Fiery reds, blacks, molten glow.

## Recommendations (build order & fidelity)

1. **Build the Heroes' Guild first** as the flagship/reference build - it has the most documented detail and is the most recognizable. Prioritize the grey-stone fortress hall, the 3D Albion map table, the chalk-stream courtyard with two bridges to the Will island, and the domed fresco Chamber of Fate. Use grey stone brick, andesite, and dark oak; cobblestone for the cave bridge; sea lanterns or soul-fire for the Cullis Gate glow.
2. **Then build the three mainland hub towns** (Bowerstone, Oakvale, Knothole Glade) to establish the three distinct architectural vocabularies: urban stone/timber (Bowerstone), coastal cottage (Oakvale), and Norse-Celtic wood (Knothole Glade).
3. **Use distinct palettes per cluster** to make regions recognizable: bright green (Greatwood), murky grey-brown fog (Darkwood), misty evergreen (Witchwood), decayed grey-green (Lychfield), and snow-white/slate-blue (Hook Coast + Northern Wastes).
4. **Model the topology as discrete zones joined by gates/paths**, mirroring the game's loading-zone structure - use distinct gateway structures (stone arches, wooden gates, Cullis Gate platforms) at each transition to preserve the original connectivity.
5. **Benchmark fidelity against screenshots/playthrough video** for each area before finalizing, since some specific material/color details could only be inferred from text descriptions, not pixel inspection. A change in scope that would justify reprioritizing: if reference screenshots reveal materials inconsistent with the textual descriptions here (e.g., roof colors, stone tints), update the per-cluster palette guidance accordingly before committing large builds.

## Caveats
- Much of the descriptive detail derives from fan wikis (Fable Wiki/Fandom, Neoseeker, GameBanshee, StrategyWiki) whose location prose is sometimes written in an in-universe/guidebook voice rather than as literal asset descriptions; treat layout facts as reliable and atmospheric flourishes as interpretive.
- Banners and stained glass for the Guild are NOT source-confirmed for the TLC version; torch/lamp lighting is implied but builders should treat decorative heraldry as discretionary rather than documented canon.
- Exact building counts and proportions vary between the childhood and adult/rebuilt versions of Oakvale, and between the original Fable and the TLC/Anniversary versions; this report describes the TLC-era state where possible.
- Some specifics (precise stone colors, roof materials) were inferred from textual sources rather than direct image analysis; verify against in-game screenshots before final builds.
- The Fable Anniversary (2014) remaster altered some vistas (e.g., Snowspire's horizon shows trees/mountains instead of the Necropolis); this report targets the 2005 TLC appearance.
- This report covers the major and minor regions confirmed across multiple sources; a handful of tiny transition sub-zones (e.g., Picnic Area, Barrow Fields, Darkwood Weir interiors) are noted by name but have minimal documented visual detail and should be treated as small connective filler areas.
